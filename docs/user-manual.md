# Using Pluggz

A short guide to the whole platform. For Lisa, Rachel and Ethan.

---

## What Pluggz does, in one paragraph

A creator posts a link to a product they like. Someone taps it, lands on the
brand's own website, and buys. The brand tells Pluggz that the sale happened.
Pluggz works out the commission, waits until the order can no longer be
returned, invoices the brand, and pays the creator their share. You do not have
to watch any of it happen.

---

## The journey of one sale

1. **A creator shares their link.** Every creator has their own page on Pluggz,
   and every product on it has its own link.
2. **A shopper taps it** and lands on the brand's website, on that product.
3. **They buy.** The brand's shop tells Pluggz, usually within a minute.
4. **Pluggz splits the commission** between the creator and Pluggz, using the
   rate agreed with that brand.
5. **The sale waits** until the brand's returns window has passed, so nothing is
   paid out on an order that comes back.
6. **Pluggz invoices the brand** for the commission.
7. **The brand pays**, and the sale is marked settled.
8. **The creator is paid** on the 1st or the 15th, whichever comes first.

Steps 4 to 8 happen on their own.

## Three kinds of login

| Who | What they see |
| --- | --- |
| **You** | Everything. Creators, brands, products, money, and the settings behind it all |
| **A creator** | Their own page, their own links, what they have earned, and how to get paid |
| **A brand** | Their own performance and invoices, and nothing about anybody else |

A brand can never see another brand's figures. A creator can never see another
creator's earnings. That is not a setting you have to remember to switch on.

## What you actually do

Most days, nothing. These are the things that need you.

**Approving a creator.** Someone applies, you look at them, you approve or
decline. Approving does not put them on the homepage; that is a separate
decision on the Homepage screen.

**Adding a brand.** Fill in the form, including the commission rate and how long
their returns window is. When you save, Pluggz gives you either a small piece of
code or a key and a secret, depending on what their shop runs on. Send that to
the brand and they install it. That is how their sales reach us.

**Adding a product.** Paste the address of the product page. Pluggz reads the
name, price and picture from it.

**Setting the rate.** Each brand has its own commission rate. It is split
between the creator and Pluggz automatically.

**Recording a payment that did not come through Stripe.** If a brand pays you by
bank transfer, open Brand invoices and record it with the bank reference. That
releases the creators behind that invoice to be paid.

Everything else, including chasing what brands owe and paying the creators,
happens without being asked.

## The Money screen

This is the one worth learning. It shows three flows, and each one shows both
what has moved and what has not.

**In, from the brands.** What they have paid, what has been invoiced and not yet
paid, and what has been earned but not yet billed.

**Out, to the creators.** What has been paid to them, and what is owed on the
next run.

**Pluggz's own share.** What the company has earned, how much has reached the
company bank account, and how much is still on its way.

Each figure sits beside its unfinished half deliberately. A screen that only
showed money received would look healthy while a brand had quietly stopped
paying.

## How creators get paid

A creator sets this up once, themselves. They open **Payouts** on their own
dashboard and press **Set up payouts**, which takes them to Stripe.

They enter their name, address, date of birth and bank details **on Stripe's
pages, not on Pluggz**. We never see those details and never store them. That is
the whole reason for using Stripe: a platform that never sees a bank account
cannot lose one.

After that, they are paid automatically on the 1st and the 15th, for any sale
whose brand has settled.

If Stripe is not ready to pay somebody, the payout is held rather than sent, and
the reason is written next to their name on the Payouts screen.

## If something looks wrong

**A creator says they have not been paid.** Open Payouts and find them. Either
their sales are still waiting for the brand to settle, or Stripe is waiting for
a document from them. The reason is on the screen.

**A brand says they have not had an invoice.** Open Brand invoices. If they are
not listed as ready to bill, their sales are still inside their returns window.

**A product looks wrong on the site.** Tell us what you were looking at and what
you expected. A screenshot and the address in the browser bar is enough.

**Nothing has changed for several days.** Tell us. We can see straight away
whether the overnight jobs have been running.
